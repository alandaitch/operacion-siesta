# The photograph

Everything in this game is modelled from one reference photograph of a real apartment in Buenos
Aires. You will not be given the image file — this document *is* the reference. Read it like a
set dresser reading a location report. Specificity is the whole job: a generic "modern living
room" is an instant fail. This room has a particular, slightly scruffy, lived-in, design-literate
personality, and it currently has a baby living in it.

**The photograph is taken from above and behind**, from roughly 2.3 m up — the top of a staircase
or a mezzanine — looking down and forward into the room toward a window wall. That high angle is
why the ceiling is such a large part of the frame. The `hero` shot reproduces it.

---

## The ceiling — the single most characterful surface in the room

Raw **board-formed concrete slab**, left exposed and unpainted, whitewashed unevenly. This is not
smooth precast: you can read the **timber formwork boards**, roughly 30 cm wide, running across
the room, each leaving a faint recessed seam and a slightly different tone. Across the slab there
are **large irregular dark grey blotches** — efflorescence, damp staining, patches where the
release agent took differently. They are organic, cloudy, high-contrast against the chalky white,
concentrated more heavily toward the near half of the ceiling and toward the window edge. There
are small pinholes, tie-rod marks, and a hairline crack or two.

Fixings: a **single bare bulb on a black fabric cord** hanging about 1.1 m down, off-centre, with
a small black ceiling rose. Nothing else — no downlights, no track.

Where the ceiling meets the window wall it **steps down** into a shallow downstand beam.

**If this ceiling looks like flat grey paint, the whole render dies.** It needs: broad low-frequency
stain mottling, medium-frequency board seams, high-frequency aggregate/pinhole grain, and a
normal map that actually catches the raking light from the window.

## The floor

Warm mid-brown, wide, very matte — reads as either a smoothed cement/microcement screed or very
wide dark-honey planks with subtle long seams. Slight sheen only where the window light rakes
across it near the glazing. Dust and micro-scuffs. Not glossy, not laminate-shiny.

## The window wall (far end)

Full-height **black-framed glazing**, two big panes plus a sliding door section, mullions slim and
matte black. Behind the glass: a shallow **balcony/terrace** with a planter of scrappy green
foliage, a black railing/grille, then **bare winter trees**, then a **red-brick apartment
building** a few metres away with its own windows. The light coming through is bright, cool-ish
daylight but low in the sky — it lands on the floor and the rug in long soft rectangles.

Below the glazing, a **white steel column radiator**.

**Sheer white curtains**: floor-to-ceiling, very fine, translucent. One panel is gathered into a
narrow vertical bundle at the left edge of the glazing, one hangs open and flat across the right
third, another gathers at the far right. Where the sun hits them they glow — real light transmission,
not just white geometry. The folds are soft, irregular, catenary-ish, and they pool very slightly
at the floor.

## The left wall — the shelving run

A long, **low, modular open-cube shelving unit** in pale birch/pine plywood runs almost the entire
left wall. It is not a single tidy bookcase: it is a **staircase of stacked cubes at three
different heights**, some units sitting on the floor, some stacked two-high, creating a stepped
silhouette. Exposed plywood edges (you can see the laminations). It looks slightly self-built.

On and in it, left to right:
- A **big framed artwork leaning against the wall** on top of the leftmost unit: an abstract on
  off-white, with a **soft yellow triangular wedge** and a **magenta/violet rounded blob with
  darker speckles**, thin pale wood frame. It leans back a few degrees.
- A **white ceramic vase**, tall-ish, with an irregular ridged/knobbly surface.
- Books lying flat, books standing, **magazines**, a small stack tipping over.
- **Vinyl records** stored vertically in one cube.
- **Two black bookshelf speakers / studio monitors**, one on a shelf mid-run, another to its right,
  slightly toed in, with visible woofer cones and a small port.
- A **small ceramic mug** and a **tiny bottle** on the top surface near the middle.
- At the far end (nearest the viewer) a **black espresso machine** with a chrome portafilter,
  sitting on a lower unit, mostly cut off by the frame edge.

## The seating — right side

A very large **modular sectional sofa** in a cream/oatmeal velvety fabric (chenille or a soft
velvet), with **deep, soft, slightly collapsed seat cushions** and low chunky arms. It runs down
the right wall and turns. Two **navy blue cushions**: one is a **ribbed/corduroy lumbar cushion**
propped in the corner, one flatter. A **dark laptop, closed or half-open, sits on the seat** —
that's the most obviously knock-off-able object in the room.

A **cream bouclé armchair** near the window: rounded, chunky, tub-shaped, on a low base, turned
about 24° toward the room.

A large **bouclé ottoman/bench** — a rounded rectangular puff, same fabric family — sitting between
the armchair and the shelving.

A smaller **cylindrical bouclé pouf** near the sofa.

Bouclé must read as bouclé: a dense field of little wool loops. A smooth cream box is a fail.

## The tables

- **Glass coffee table**: entirely transparent, thick low-iron glass, a slab top on two glass
  side panels (waterfall form). It has green-ish edges where you see the glass thickness. It
  refracts and reflects the room. There is a small dark object on it.
- **Round side table**: small white marble or ceramic top on a slim stem, near the sofa arm.

## The rug

Large **cream/ivory wool rug** covering most of the seating zone. Short dense pile with a visible
weave direction and a soft sheen that shifts with view angle. It is not perfectly flat — it rucks
slightly and its edge lifts a little where furniture sits on it.

## The playpen — foreground, and the biggest object in frame

A **large square baby playpen**, roughly 2.6–2.8 m a side, dominating the near half of the room and
sitting on the rug/floor. Construction: a **padded tubular frame in a warm beige/gold quilted
fabric**, with **white mesh panels** that are genuinely see-through — you can read the rug and the
mat right through them, and they catch light as a soft grey-white veil. There is a **zippered door
panel** on the near face with a rectangular mesh window and a fabric flap, and small **plastic
adjusters and green webbing tabs** on the frame. A **silicone teether ring** dangles from one rail.

Inside:
- A **padded play mat** in white/pale grey with soft pale-blue and sage shapes printed on it.
- A **white muslin blanket** bunched into a heap in one corner.
- A **navy blue cushion**.
- A **baby play gym**: a soft fabric arch over a padded round mat, with toys hanging from it —
  a small grey elephant, rings, a bird.
- A **big pile of plush toys**: bunnies, a teddy, a grey mouse, a giraffe, assorted rattles,
  a colourful board book, a **pink/red box**, small stacking cups, a **wooden toy ukulele** with a
  pale body and dark fretboard lying on its side.

The toys are the only saturated colours in the entire frame. That contrast is the composition.

## Plants and lamps

- A **monstera** in a pale pot to the right of the window: 8–10 big fenestrated leaves on long
  petioles, leaning toward the light, one leaf drooping.
- A second smaller **leafy plant** beside it.
- A **floor lamp** at the far right against the curtain: a slim stem with a **cream mushroom-dome
  shade** that glows warm.
- Outside on the balcony, a low planter of greenery.

## Miscellaneous, and important

- An **orange/amber foil snack bag** lying on the rug near the sofa. This is the thing the baby
  most obviously wants to eat.
- A **rattan/cane chair or basket** clipped by the bottom-right corner of the frame.
- Nothing in this room is square to anything else. The rug is a few degrees off the walls, the
  armchair is turned, books lean, cushions are dented, the ottoman is pushed out of line. **Every
  object should carry a small random rotation and a plausible reason for being where it is.**
- The room is *tidy-ish but not styled*. There is a laptop on the sofa, a snack bag on the rug,
  a blanket in a heap. Real people live here and one of them is very small.

## The light, precisely

Late afternoon, winter, overcast-bright with the sun breaking through low. The key is the entire
window wall acting as one enormous soft box, plus a harder direct component raking in low from
beyond the glazing at about 12–18° elevation, casting long soft-edged rectangles across the floor
and up the ottoman. That bounces off the cream rug into a huge warm underfill that lifts the
undersides of the furniture and washes the raw concrete ceiling. The far corners of the room fall
into a soft, cool, deep shadow. The floor lamp and the bare bulb are on but *weak* relative to
daylight — they read as small warm points, not as the key.

Colour temperature split: **cool daylight from the window, warm bounce from the room, warm
practicals**. That split is what makes the frame feel photographic.
